Redbud County Moves Mail Ballot Receipt Deadline Up Two Weeks Ahead of Fall Vote

By Curtis Wenzel, Redbud County Bureau · Published June 24, 2026

Redbud County Moves Mail Ballot Receipt Deadline Up Two Weeks Ahead of Fall Vote

The Redbud County Board of Elections voted 4-1 Tuesday to make two changes to how mailed ballots come back: it moved the receipt deadline up by two weeks, and it extended the hours on the county's in-person return options. Both take effect for the November general election.

The first change rewrites the postmark rule. Under the old rule, a ballot postmarked by Election Day and received within ten days still counted. Starting this fall, mailed ballots must physically arrive at the county elections office by 5 p.m. the Friday before Election Day, regardless of postmark.

Board members cited a backlog at the regional postal sorting facility that delayed several thousand ballots past the old ten-day window in the last general election, all of which were disqualified. “We were throwing out ballots from people who did everything we told them to do,” said Harlan Beecroft, the county's elections director. “The postmark rule stopped protecting voters a while ago.”

The second change extends return hours. All 14 of the county's drop boxes will stay open two hours later each day during the final week before the deadline, and the three sites that offer curbside return for voters with mobility needs will keep the same extended schedule. Beecroft said the county is still telling anyone voting by mail to send the ballot back at least two weeks before Election Day.

The county says the change is intended to reduce late-arriving ballots, while voter advocates are asking for clearer notice and more accessible return options.

The new deadline applies to the November general election. Voters who plan to use the mail should check the county's published calendar and consider using a drop box or an in-person return site during the final two weeks.

What changes this fall

  • Mailed ballots must arrive by 5 p.m. the Friday before Election Day.
  • All 14 county drop boxes will stay open two hours later during the final week.
  • Three curbside return sites will keep their extended schedule for voters with mobility needs.
